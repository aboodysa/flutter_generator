- `idiomLints: []` — a plugin-authored lint pack enforced by the arch linter (§21) that mechanizes the part of idiomaticity that *is* checkable (Riverpod: no `ref.watch` in build causing rebuild storms, codegen used not hand-rolled providers; Bloc: no shared-state mutation, sealed states exhaustive). This shrinks, but doesn't eliminate, the human-judgment residue.
- `goldenReference: {approvedBy, version, artifact}` — the one-time human-expert sign-off, amortized per plugin *version* the same way golden UI baselines are amortized per screen version, not paid per generated app.

The honest bottom line: idiomaticity is permanently outside *automated self-certification*, but not outside the design's trust-boundary architecture — it reuses the golden-baseline pattern instead of inventing an unaudited category. One caveat I'd add on top of your question: that expert reviewer shouldn't be the plugin's own author by default (same "who watches the watcher" issue as agent self-report) — for any plugin not maintained by the core team, the golden-reference approval should come from someone independent of whoever wrote the plugin.

**Question 5:** You just agreed the rule grammar (§19) should be monotonically extensible, with coverage measured as a release criterion per grammar version. §16.2 gives every generated app a lockfile pinning IR/plugin/template versions with an "upgrade/regen path" for major bumps — but that's scoped to plugin/template drift, not to *semantic* grammar growth. Here's the sharp case: an app ships with a rule that fell through to the §19.4 extension queue and got hand-coded as `ExternalCodeNode` — human-approved, tested, in production. Six months later, the grammar grows a new native primitive (say, weighted scoring) that structurally covers what that hand-coded workaround does. What happens to that already-deployed app's IR? Does the fleet auto-migrate, stay frozen forever on its original grammar version, or is there a reconciliation path — and if there is, does that mean §0's "IR is the single source of truth" claim actually only holds *within one grammar version*, not across a fleet of apps built over time?

My recommended answer: never auto-migrate. Grammar bumps must be strictly additive and non-retroactive with respect to already-approved Novel/extension-queue code — that code passed human approval and adversarial review, and silently superseding it with a new primitive would violate the exact "never silently reclassify" guarantee §5.4 already establishes for the Novel lane in the other direction. Instead, a grammar bump should surface a **suggestion**, not a migration: "`ext:legacy_promo_mapper` structurally matches new primitive `weightedScore`; consider replacing" — routed through the same human-approval gate as any other IR change, opt-in, never automatic. And I'd state the scoping honestly in §2.4/§16.2 rather than let it be implied: the "single source of truth" claim holds *per app, as of its last regeneration against its pinned grammar version* — it is not a live, fleet-wide invariant, and the design should say so instead of letting the phrase in §0 imply more than the mechanism delivers.
